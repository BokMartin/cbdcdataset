Institutional Determinants of Privacy Design in Central Bank Digital Currencies

Martin Bok & Dominik Stroukal

Download paper (PDF)Inspect the pipeline

Abstract

Central bank digital currencies (CBDCs) make transaction privacy an explicit institutional design choice. We ask whether political and economic conditions predict the privacy posture documented by central banks, while separating privacy vocabulary from design commitments. A frozen multilingual corpus contains 113 official or authority-attributed documents (3,963 pages) across forty-seven jurisdictions and two institutional composites. Two independently run large-language-model extractors produced 11,358 source-verified statements, deduplicated into 6,949 candidates. We aggregate them with a predeclared equal-vote ensemble in which every candidate has unit mass and provider disagreements are retained rather than averaged away. On a separate calibration set, the verified union attained precision 0.980, recall 0.766 and span fidelity 1.000; it failed the preregistered recall gate and is therefore treated as exploratory, not automated gold. In the production union, the privacy-family share and documented privacy posture correlate only r = 0.219. No tested institutional condition survives family-wise correction; the largest commitment-side association is with the informal economy (r = −0.286). A pre-frozen 5.25% double-blind human audit remains to be inserted before submission. The design demonstrates both the value and the limits of reproducible multi-model qualitative extraction.

Keywords: central bank digital currency; privacy; content analysis; large language models; reproducibility
JEL: E42, E58, C83

What the paper finds

1

How often privacy appears in official documents is a poor proxy for actual documented privacy design: the two measures share only 4.8% of their variance.

2

No tested political or economic condition is a statistically robust predictor after correcting for multiple tests.

3

The informal economy supplies the strongest posture-side lead (r = −0.286), but its interval reaches zero; it supports follow-up research, not a settled claim that high-informality economies “stomp on” privacy.

4

Two-model extraction improves coverage, but substantial provider disagreement and failed calibrated recall require explicit sensitivity analysis and sampled human validation.

Submission status

Not yet the final submitted version. The paper is nine pages and has the conference formatting corrections applied, but the results table still needs the locked double-blind human validation: design-weighted keep rate and interval, author agreement, Krippendorff’s α, Gwet’s AC1, provider-to-consensus agreement and dual-empty missed-claim yield.

The substantive analysis will continue to use the full ensemble, with OpenAI, Claude and exact-consensus variants reported as sensitivity checks. Human review estimates measurement quality; it does not replace the full production corpus with a 5% sample.
