Institutional determinants of privacy design in central bank digital currencies

We separate how often central banks talk about privacy from what their documents actually commit to. The evidence comes from a frozen multilingual corpus, two independent LLM extractors and exact source-span verification.

Method

A reproducible boundary around LLM-assisted extraction

OpenAI and Claude processed byte-identical, frozen source packages and codebooks independently. Every accepted statement required an exact source quote, a substantive code, observation/decision/recommendation status, and privacy fields where applicable. Raw provider outputs were archived before deterministic parsing.

Why the results are labelled exploratory

The verified union passed precision and span-fidelity gates but failed the predeclared recall threshold. It is analysed as a source-verified candidate dataset, not automated gold.

Human validation boundary

  • 365 of 6,949 candidates (5.2526%)
  • 36 of 359 dual-empty units
  • independent, blinded coding by both authors
  • disagreements adjudicated only after both files are hash-locked
Provider sensitivity

The ensemble is primary; each model remains visible

Consensus-only evidence is a selected high-overlap subset, not a neutral adjudicator. Provider variants show how much interpretation changes with model selection.

Plain-language interpretation

Central banks can discuss privacy frequently without documenting privacy-preserving design choices. Economies with larger informal sectors show a tendency toward less privacy-preserving commitments in this sample, but the statistical uncertainty is too large to call that a settled result. It is a justified hypothesis for a larger follow-up study.
